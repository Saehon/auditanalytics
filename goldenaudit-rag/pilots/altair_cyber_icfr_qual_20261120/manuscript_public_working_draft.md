# When Cybersecurity Disclosure Becomes Audit Evidence
**Standards-as-Code, Agentic Delegation, and the Boundaries of Professional Judgment**

*Saeid Homayoun · University of Gävle · AOS-oriented qualitative documentary pilot · Working draft 20 November 2026 · NOT SUBMISSION READY*

## Abstract
When does a regulatory cyber-risk narrative become evidence capable of supporting an internal-audit conclusion? We develop a theory of evidentiary boundary work at the intersection of regulatory applicability, technical control testing, and delegated professional authority. In a bounded documentary pilot, we analyze an official July 11, 2023 SEC letter concerning Altair Engineering Inc.'s earlier cybersecurity incident, identified in a public archive in the researcher's Google Drive and verified against the [SEC original](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1701732/000119312523185004/filename1.htm). Twelve preliminary meaning units produce four themes: regulatory translation, control-claim/test separation, accountability, and materiality/value claims. A deterministic audit standards engine applied as a 2026 *hypothetical methodology* registers twelve frameworks, selects seven sample objectives and returns NOT_TESTED_MISSING_DATA for all seven. It asserts zero control exceptions and blocks professional report release. An explicit historical 2023 re-run selects no objectives from IIA requirements that were not effective then. This pilot makes no claims of actual ICFR weakness, independently established cyber materiality, successful autonomous multi-agent auditing, or validated professional judgment. It proposes an embedded field study to investigate how auditors negotiate evidence and delegation boundaries.

## Theoretical framing
AOS ethnography documents how professional networks translate IFRS requirements into situated judgments (Kohler, Pochet, & Gendron, 2021). Regulatory meaning is shaped through interactions of intermediaries and affected organizations rather than static textual matching (Kohler, Pochet, & Le Manh, 2021). Ahrens and Chapman (2006) emphasize positioning accounting practices to build theory. Complementary sociomaterial studies examine audit analytics' effects on professional work (Salijeni et al., 2021; Samiolo, Spence, & Toh, 2023). Baird and Maruping (2021) conceptualize delegation to and from agentic information systems. Together these motivate three boundaries: **authority eligibility**, **test-evidence sufficiency**, and **professional authorization**. A future empirical claim would be that preserving these boundaries reorganizes how auditors exercise skeptical judgment. That proposition is untested.

## Case and documentary method
The historical source is one public SEC correspondence file (accession 0001193125-23-185004). It documents a SEC question regarding hypothetical cyber-risk framing following an earlier incident, along with an issuer description of its response and materiality reasoning. It is **not** an internal-audit file. Twelve preliminary, AI-assisted qualitative units are openly logged, with actor attribution, SEC line references, first-order codes and epistemic limits. One is an analyst cross-reading, ten issuer statements or positions, and one a regulator question. Four provisional themes (3/4/3/2 units) are not generalizable statistics. There is no second coder, saturation claim, observation or interview.

## Findings and bounded technical pilot
**Regulatory translation (3 units):** The SEC challenges hypothetical disclosure in light of a historical event; the issuer disputes the disclosure inference. The result is documented interpretive contention, not established noncompliance.

**Claims versus control tests (4 units):** An incident timeline and the issuer's opinion that controls were effective do not supply verified access-review populations, ERP logs, or independent operating-effectiveness workpapers. All seven 2026-selected illustrative control tests are NOT_TESTED_MISSING_DATA; absence of exceptions must not be read as evidence of effective control.

**Accountability (3 units):** Board consideration and future-disclosure intentions are reported but minutes, independent reviewer determinations and signed audit evidence are not provided. The prototype blocks any professional conclusion with eight software-governance blockers, none an audited organizational deficiency.

**Value claims (2 units):** The issuer describes remediation costs of less than USD 200,000 and no effect on revenues, impairments or cash flows. These remain issuer claims. IIRC financial, intellectual and relationship capitals can generate questions but were not directly measured or causally estimated.

The research program executed **46 pre-existing plus seven new unit tests**, all passing. Applying the post-2025/26 IIA framework to a hypothetical 2026 engagement selects seven objectives; re-running with a July 2023 as-of date selects zero: a version-gating software result, not a change in the issuer's compliance. SOX scope was not resolved and PCAOB AS 2201 was treated as external-audit reference. No LLM evaluation A0–A4 was run.

## Interpretation, rival explanations, and AOS fit
The substantive proposal is that professional auditability depends on processes that authorize movement from an organizational account to a conclusion. An **evidence refusal** may be a productive organizational control, not an AI failure. However, the missing evidence here could simply reflect the public-document genre, while actual auditors may possess richer internal records. The eight programmatic release blockers reflect the software specification. No claim can be made about the issuer's audit governance beyond its own statements. AOS would require sustained organizational fieldwork and theoretical integration, not one letter.

A comparative study should seek 3–5 organizations, 20–30 semi-structured interviews (targets only), observed review/clearance sessions, authorized de-identified RCMs, workpapers, agent traces and negative cases. Temporal bracketing could follow: engagement scope → evidence request → AI recommendation → human challenge → report release. Rival propositions concern mere documentation standardization versus a change in epistemic authority. Independent analysis and reflexive audit trails will be required before empirical theory claims.

## Limitations and research status
One documentary source, no interviews, no external ICFR/ITGC evidence, no adjudicators, no dual coding or expert-gold answers, no model-arm evaluation and no evidence of an actual financial misstatement. Numerical software results are deterministic checks, not evidence of superior audit performance. This public working draft is a companion to the longer 12-page Word manuscript; it is not a journal submission.
