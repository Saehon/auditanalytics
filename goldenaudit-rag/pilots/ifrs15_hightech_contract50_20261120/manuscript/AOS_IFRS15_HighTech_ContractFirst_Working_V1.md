# From High-Tech Customer Contracts to Auditability: Revenue Judgments, Agentic Evidence Boundaries and the Organizational Translation of Risk

**Accounting, Organizations and Society — qualitative working manuscript, Version 1.0 (20 November 2026)**  
**Research status:** Empirical *feasibility pilot*, not a completed contract sample, validated multi-agent experiment, or submission-ready paper.  
**Principal investigator:** Saeid Homayoun, University of Gävle, Sweden.  
**Keywords:** IFRS 15; ASC 606; customer contracts; software licensing; SaaS; auditability; internal control over financial reporting; critical audit matters; agentic AI; documentary analysis; accounting judgment.

## Abstract

Contract analysis has become increasingly machine-readable, but its translation into professionally defensible accounting and audit judgments remains an organizational problem. We develop a contract-first research design for high-technology customer arrangements, asking how promised software licences, hosted services, support, variable consideration and principal–agent relationships become—or fail to become—recognizable accounting obligations, internal-control risks and public audit matters. We conceptualize **evidentiary boundary work** as the professional effort required to prevent a contractual cue, an accounting judgment, a control hypothesis and an audit report from being treated as interchangeable evidence. The research architecture integrates a versioned Standards-as-Code knowledge core, a replaceable agentic technology core, independent evidence verification and mandatory human authorization, alongside a six-capital value perspective. A preliminary documentary feasibility test examines three authentic SEC comment-response files from high-technology issuers: Lightspeed Commerce (IFRS 15), Cloudera (ASC 606), and National Instruments (ASC 606). Ten source-located passages were provisionally coded across performance-obligation distinctness, licence/service separation, recognition timing, stand-alone selling prices, principal–agent judgment and control disclosure. The observed correspondence exposes substantive questions and management explanations but cannot, by itself, establish the underlying contractual terms, control operating effectiveness, CAM identity or actual financial impact. We accordingly provide an executable, non-fabricating selection and screening protocol targeting the first 50 real high-tech contracts from the publicly released CUAD corpus, conditional on verified access and source review. No 50-contract analysis or agent-comparison experiment has yet occurred. The study's theoretical promise is to explain how bounded AI-supported interpretation may shift—not eliminate—the work of constructing auditability. Empirical contribution remains contingent on a verified contract sample, public-document triangulation and independent human coding.

## 1. Introduction

High-technology businesses sell combinations of hosted software, licences, intellectual property, recurring maintenance, implementation services, integration and transaction processing. Such arrangements distribute rights and obligations over time and across firms. A customer agreement may simultaneously contain economic promises, pricing contingencies and operational dependencies, yet the resulting accounting does not follow from keyword detection alone. Under IFRS 15, the determination of a contract, distinct performance obligations, transaction price, allocation and satisfaction of obligations depends on a particular reporting entity and fact pattern (IFRS Foundation, 2026). Revenue recognition for a licence can differ from revenue for ongoing access or support. Determining whether a software reseller is a principal or agent similarly requires judgment about the specified goods and services and control. These decisions are not established by the agreement title or by an AI-generated label.

The organizational significance is broader than technical accounting. Contracts enter accounting systems through legal, commercial and operational routines. Their interpretation must be stabilized in a revenue memo, reconciled to ERP records, governed by controls, challenged by auditors and sometimes described within a critical audit matter (CAM). A cyber incident or third-party system disruption may undermine evidence supporting fulfilment without necessarily creating a financial misstatement or ICFR material weakness. Conversely, an accounting judgment may become significant in the absence of a security event. The six-capital framework invites additional questions about intellectual, human, manufactured, natural and relationship resources, but it does not transform such questions into independently demonstrated value losses.

Qualitative accounting research has established that auditability is socially produced rather than merely discovered (Power, 1996). Kohler, Pochet and Le Manh (2021) show how auditors participate in the interpretation of IFRS 15 in an industry setting. Kohler, Pochet and Gendron (2021) study networks of interpretation within an accounting firm. These works reveal that standards' meaning does not flow automatically from stable text to a client-specific accounting answer. Recent AOS work on verification and organizational repair further cautions against treating assurance as a unitary, mechanical practice (Adams, Hall, & Xiao, 2024). The emergence of multi-agent AI raises a new question: if standards applicability and clause interpretation are partially delegated to computational agents, where is the epistemic boundary between source retrieval, professional judgment, and authorized conclusions?

Our proposed answer is *evidentiary boundary work*: the traceable effort of keeping distinct the meanings and evidentiary force of (i) customer contract text; (ii) organizational claims about that text; (iii) accounting policy or measurement; (iv) control design and operating evidence; (v) auditor judgments and CAM communication; and (vi) enterprise value claims. This is not a claim that multi-agent architecture improves audit outcomes. Rather, the architecture provides a designed research setting in which such distinctions can be made visible, questioned and empirically tested.

This working paper contributes a reproducible documentary pilot and a prespecified path to a 50-contract qualitative research sample. The sample target is not represented as accomplished. We use three genuine issuer SEC correspondence records to expose the conceptual differentiation, but we explicitly do not call those letters customer contracts. This decision prioritizes evidence integrity over illustrative completeness.

### Research questions

**RQ1.** How are high-tech customer commitments translated from original agreement clauses into IFRS 15/ASC 606 accounting judgments and organizational control responsibilities?

**RQ2.** When an agentic audit system performs standards screening and clause extraction, how are knowledge authority, evidentiary sufficiency, contradiction and professional decision rights allocated?

**RQ3.** Under what documentary conditions can the same contract-specific issue be linked to an accounting note, an ICFR assessment and a CAM—and when is an apparent link merely topical coincidence?

**RQ4.** How do unobserved or missing evidence and divergent accounting jurisdictions interrupt proposed extensions into cybersecurity and the six-capital value model?

## 2. Theoretical framing

### 2.1 Contract interpretation as a collective accounting accomplishment

The existence of a signed agreement is not equivalent to evidence that every promised activity is a distinct performance obligation. IFRS 15 asks how a customer can benefit from specified goods or services and whether the promise is separately identifiable; it also distinguishes right-to-use from right-to-access licences under relevant fact patterns. Kohler, Pochet and Le Manh (2021) demonstrate that seemingly technical requirements acquire practical meaning through mediation among auditors, clients and standard setters. In the high-tech sector, this is especially consequential because a bundled subscription may be described simultaneously as software, access, implementation, support or a commercial solution. A qualitative account must therefore follow the documentary and organizational process by which these descriptions are reconciled.

An agent can retrieve clauses, produce a provisional obligations matrix and identify conflicting accounting treatments. It cannot, from a generic commercial contract alone, determine the reporting policy, promised customer outputs or materiality of the resulting revenue. Apparent certainty may reflect the system's completion of a template rather than completion of professional judgment. Accordingly, our contract-first design treats machine-generated classifications as occasions for inquiry, not as accounting results.

### 2.2 Making things auditable: bounded evidence versus complete stories

Power (1996) theorizes how knowledge objects are configured to become susceptible to recognized verification. This implies that an ERP control and a public auditor report are distinct regimes of visibility. A CAM is not a comprehensive list of risk exposures: the PCAOB definition links communication to material accounts or disclosures and especially challenging, subjective or complex auditor judgment (PCAOB, AS 3101). Equally, a public disclosure that management considers a control effective does not substitute for a test of design and operation, and absence of a disclosed material weakness cannot be coded as zero risk without an issuer-year source review.

The proposed agentic system therefore records a series of gates. A clause is *identified* when a source span is retrieved and anchored. It is *accounting-relevant* when linked to a specific assertion and conditional interpretation. It becomes a *control hypothesis* when a control objective and evidence requirement are specified. It becomes a *tested control finding* only after genuine control evidence supports a tested result. It becomes a *verified CAM alignment* only when the original auditor report supports the same issuer-year accounting issue. These transitions must be observable; otherwise, the apparently continuous value chain is an analyst-created narrative.

### 2.3 Delegation and the relocation of professional skepticism

Baird and Maruping (2021) theorize delegation between human actors and agentic information systems. In audit practice, delegation may reduce search and documentation costs while increasing risks of misplaced authority or routinized acceptance. Experimental accounting work by Commerford et al. (2022) establishes that reliance on AI in complex estimates requires attention to how auditor judgment is exercised, while further evidence on auditor input and AI reliance (Commerford et al., 2024) complicates simplistic assumptions about automation. This project operationalizes a rule: no agent may approve its own material output. Knowledge retrieval and evidence verification are intentionally separated; professional authorization is a substantive boundary, not a decorative interface control.

### 2.4 From compliance to value: do not collapse six capitals

The Integrated Reporting Framework distinguishes financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social and relationship, and natural capital. A software subscription may generate intellectual capital, create dependence on a third-party platform, or affect customer relationships. Such statements remain prospective mechanisms until grounded in measured consequences. The right-hand value brain must preserve the outcome measure, unit, period, counterfactual and stakeholder for each capital. It must not add incompatible six-capital quantities into one unsupported risk score. This demarcation allows a future inquiry into whether professional reviewers perceive the system as useful for learning and organizational repair, without presuming causality (Adams et al., 2024).

## 3. Research setting, material and method

### 3.1 Source hierarchy and case definition

The **target unit of analysis** is the contract–issuer–fiscal-year–accounting-issue, not a keyword, source paragraph, SEC correspondence record or AI response. A customer contract is admissible only if the authentic agreement or valid SEC exhibit is available, the contracting parties and reporting entity can be identified, customer consideration is plausible, the contractual chronology is established, and the governing accounting basis is independently determined. If a reporting entity uses US GAAP, ASC 606—not IFRS 15—is its operative standard; conceptual comparison to IFRS 15 must be disclosed. For older agreements the effective date of the standard and the accounting period must also be considered.

The intended evidence hierarchy starts from SEC original Exhibit 10 agreements and amendments. The free CUAD corpus (510 annotated legal agreements) supplies an accessible discovery and clause-retrieval benchmark but its 41 expert legal labels are not IFRS performance-obligation adjudications. Kaggle mirrors and third-party hosted copies may facilitate discovery, but overlap cannot be counted as new contracts. The initially proposed sample will comprise the first **50 independently screened high-tech customer-relevant contract candidates** selected by the supplied deterministic ranking routine from the rights-cleared CUAD source, after review of title, vendor/customer role and original SEC filing. The algorithm is a *candidate-selection aid* and its deterministic selection may privilege contracts with repeated technology vocabulary; purposive qualitative sampling must document and correct this bias.

Because a raw CUAD JSON copy was not accessible to the local research runtime on the execution date, **no 50 actual contracts were read, coded or matched to CAM/ICFR**. The protocol reports this source-access limitation and contains no fabricated 50-row empirical table. A reproducible runner is provided with explicit failure/shortfall reporting, hash-based deduplication and a field that records each missing professional verification. A separate local synthetic SQuAD-shaped fixture is used solely for software unit tests.

### 3.2 Executed documentary feasibility pilot

Three public SEC CORRESP documents already present in the researcher's Drive were inspected through authenticated retrieval. These are regulatory comment-response materials that discuss software revenue accounting, not their underlying sales contracts. Selection is purposive rather than statistical: one explicit IFRS 15 foreign-issuer case (Lightspeed Commerce) and two US GAAP ASC 606 technology issuers (Cloudera and National Instruments) were chosen because they make different interpretive disputes visible. Each document has a firm CIK, accession identifier and filing date. Source-located passages were coded using document character positions from the accessible text representation, allowing later rechecking against the original SEC text (although the retrieved representation may include HTML entities and extraction effects).

The ten source-located units are **preliminary single-researcher analytic paraphrases**, not independent expert coding or AI-generated expert gold. The evidence chain is documented in an accompanying CSV; no confidential client evidence or full protected accounting standard text is published. A second coder, reflexive memo process, rival reading and independent reinspection of SEC original filings are required before making qualitative findings that depend on subtle interpretive distinctions.

### 3.3 Qualitative coding approach

The initial sensitizing framework distinguishes five accounting mechanisms: distinctness and licence/service separation; transaction-price allocation and standalone selling price; timing of revenue recognition; principal–agent analysis; and ICFR governance or disclosure. Each passage also receives an evidence-status code: regulator question; management assertion; independently verified contract fact; operating-control evidence; auditor CAM text; or unavailable. The provisional register currently contains regulator questions and management assertions only. Absence of a term in these correspondence files is coded *not observed in this source*, not *absent from the company*.

A proposed Gioia-style display may be produced after independent coding: first-order documentary interpretations; second-order accounting/control boundary practices; aggregate dimensions of interpretive delegation, evidence conversion, temporal repair and authorization. This is a suggested presentation strategy rather than a claim that grounded theory emerged from three documents. We complement this with within-case temporal bracketing and explicit negative cases. Cases that show an IFRS/ASC judgment without a CAM or a control disclosure without a contract-level trace are theoretically necessary to avoid tautological linkage.

### 3.4 Agentic architecture as object and instrument

The system preserves a stable Standards/Professional Knowledge Core and replaceable Technology Core, operationalized through four implementation areas: Knowledge, Science, Technology, and Adversarial/Digital Twin. Its Assurance Brain handles IFRS/ASC interpretation, ICFR, IIA internal audit, cybersecurity, third-party control risk and CAM/KAM contextualization; its Value Brain examines economic outcomes and six IIRC capitals. The POMELO router, VERA evidence passports, DARWIN independent contradiction checks and GoldenAudit holdout evaluator are *designed system components*. Current deterministic code screens candidate text and applies fail-closed flags; it does not invoke a live GPT/Claude/Gemini multi-agent audit workflow on customer contracts.

The future A0–A4 comparison isolates closed-book LLM, hybrid retrieval, independent verification, specialist agent coordination and optional graph retrieval. No such comparison is claimed or estimated in this manuscript. Cases must be independently adjudicated before reporting supported-and-authorized answer rates. Contract workpapers, evaluation keys and model outputs must be separated to prevent contamination. This is not a test of firm financial reporting quality unless reconciled to actual enterprise records and independently reviewed.

This result is a methodological checkpoint: the current empirical foundation permits a comparative reading of regulatory correspondence but not a finding about IFRS compliance, misstatement, CAMs or control effectiveness. The distinction between planned 50 contracts and inspected three correspondence files is fundamental, not a technical footnote.

### 4.2 Lightspeed Commerce: obligations and control over services

In its March 2022 filing (accession 0001823306-22-000022), the SEC staff asked how the company differentiated software subscriptions from legacy subscription licences and invoked IFRS 15 requirements concerning distinct obligations and licence treatment. The company responded that the offerings were separately characterized, described the hosted service arrangements and addressed revenue recognized over time. A distinct SEC question focused on why payment-processing services were recorded gross and how the company determined that it was the principal. These documentary exchanges make the *justification of an accounting boundary* observable. They do not disclose all customer agreements, the transaction-level population or audit test results. The source therefore supports interpretive questions, not a conclusion that gross presentation was correct or that controls failed.

A proposed future contract-level code is `IFRS15_PO_DISTINCTNESS` with a separate `IFRS15_PRINCIPAL_AGENT` code. The evidentiary question is whether an original agreement gives the vendor control over the specified service and whether the promise is distinct. Neither conclusion can be inferred solely from the company's response to regulators. A cyber or third-party control question may arise for hosted services, but no security incident is observed or inferred from this source.

### 4.3 Cloudera: licence/support differentiation and a separate ICFR disclosure conversation

The August 2020 Cloudera correspondence (accession 0001628280-20-012941) addresses software revenue under ASC 606. The company described treating proprietary software licences, where present, and support as separate performance obligations, recognizing licence revenue at a point in time while describing stand-ready support over time. Separately, SEC staff asked management to explicitly disclose its conclusion regarding ICFR effectiveness in Item 9A. The co-occurrence of these exchanges within the same regulator-response document is analytically useful: it shows the revenue judgment and disclosure-governance questions can inhabit the same reporting encounter. It **does not prove** that a software obligation caused an ICFR weakness or that a CAM existed. Such a claim would require a verified original Item 9A, auditor report, relevant account-year and causal/issue-specific evidence.

The case illustrates how a system that simply links a revenue keyword to an ICFR keyword can generate a false causal story. A disciplined agent must record `ASC606_REVENUE_QUERY` and `ICFR_DISCLOSURE_QUERY` as independent evidence objects until an authenticated common control mechanism is demonstrated. This is a particularly informative negative control for the future multi-agent benchmark: a compliant output should refuse to equate proximity in a document with relation in an audit.

### 4.4 National Instruments: transaction-price allocation as interpretive work

In the June 2019 correspondence (accession 0000935494-19-000024), SEC staff asked how the company established standalone selling price for software licences commonly sold with maintenance. The company explained its use of observed maintenance renewals and consistent pricing relationships. The account illuminates the informational difficulty of allocating bundled consideration where standalone licence prices are not routinely observable. It does not establish the reliability of the pricing evidence or an audit exception. The related IFRS 15 consideration is the allocation of a contract transaction price to distinct performance obligations based on relative standalone selling prices, but the operative accounting basis of the issuer was ASC 606.

For a future original contract, the test would reconstruct the promised licence and maintenance services, payment schedule, observable prices, pricing methodology and supporting commercial evidence. Only after obtaining the underlying contract and financial records could the system perform a bounded allocation or compare it to reported revenue. Because neither source is present, `S4_ALLOCATION_CANDIDATE` is a descriptive code, not an accuracy result.

### 4.5 Cross-case interpretation, competing readings and negative evidence

Across the three letters, revenue-accounting disputes are not reducible to a single variable called “contract complexity.” The visible institutional activities differ: Lightspeed concerns how subscription and payment-processing promises are categorized; Cloudera concerns licence/support disaggregation as well as an independent governance-disclosure query; National Instruments concerns estimation of allocation prices. One plausible interpretation is that regulator questions express ambiguity demanding clarification. A rival interpretation is that they are routine disclosure dialogue with limited economic consequence. Nothing in the inspected records resolves that distinction. Nor can public correspondence reveal how internal auditors, accounting experts and AI agents actually interact inside the organization.

The methodological implication is nonetheless sharp. An automated system must differentiate the existence of a question from the validity of its answer, a management explanation from independent evidence, and a documentation request from a finding of control weakness. In each case the compliant system disposition is *refer for further evidence*, not *automatic deficiency*. The current software tests support only that the screening code preserves such missing-evidence states in synthetic fixtures; they do not show observed reduction of real professional errors.

## 5. Proposed 50-contract qualitative study and joins

### 5.1 Recruitment and screening sequence

First, freeze a rights-cleared CUAD source archive and record its checksum, original authorship, licence and train/test partitions. Deduplicate title/context hashes. The executable program produces a deterministic technology-relevance candidate ranking and outputs at most 50 *actual parsed* agreements. It never creates missing rows. Second, independently inspect each candidate's agreement title, contractual parties, revenue-generating customer relationship, issuer industry, date and reporting framework. Titles such as technology services, licensing, reseller, distribution and development are inclusion candidates; employment, financing and security agreements are presumptive exclusions unless selected as theoretically informative contrasts. Third, verify original SEC Exhibit 10 document and amendments, accession, legal entity/CIK and fiscal year. Fourth, resolve actual IFRS 15 or US GAAP ASC 606 application. Without those checks, the entry remains a candidate and should not enter the main 50-case analytic sample.

A target theoretical matrix seeks SaaS/hosting, licence/IP, reseller/distribution, implementation/development and multicomponent support/maintenance arrangements, with positive and deviant examples within each type. Ten per stratum is an initial planning target, never a requirement to fill unavailable strata. Record missing, unverifiable and multiple-issue cases without substituting synthetic observations. Stop with an explicit shortfall if fewer than 50 satisfy the conditions. Do not use CAM or disclosed weaknesses to decide which contracts enter the initial sample.

### 5.2 Contract → accounting → CAM → ICFR → risk and capitals

For each admitted contract, establish: Stage 1 identification of customer and enforceability; Stage 2 promised outputs and distinctness; Stage 3 fixed/variable consideration; Stage 4 allocation to obligations; Stage 5 control transfer and timing. Each step produces a source-located conditional conclusion and explicit unknowns. A separate risk-control matrix then connects the accounting assertion to management review, pricing/SSP approval, contract modifications, ERP billing and performance/acceptance evidence. Only actual record populations, process evidence and tested controls can change a `HYPOTHESIS` label to `TESTED`.

The CAM comparison requires an original auditor report from a relevant issuer fiscal year, and the same issue—not merely the word revenue—to be independently verified. The possible outcomes are exact issue match, issue related but aggregated, topic-only mention, verified no relevant CAM, CAM not applicable and unknown. The ICFR layer uses contemporaneous Item 9A and/or auditor ICFR reporting, documenting related weakness, unrelated weakness, verified no disclosed weakness, remediation, not-in-scope and unknown. This distinction prevents falsely coding “no disclosure” as “no risk.”

Cyber and third-party dependencies are screened only when a contract assigns access, data hosting, processing or confidentiality responsibilities. Such clauses define possible control tests; they do not prove cyber incidents. Six-capital impacts are coded only as hypotheses until evidence identifies affected resources and measures. Financial effects, IP protection, employee skills, social trust, manufactured capacity and natural resource demands cannot be collapsed into an unexplained score.

### 5.3 Comparative qualitative validation and agent evaluation

Two independent domain-qualified researchers should code a subset of admitted contracts blind to system outputs, preserve disagreements and adjudicate before any case-level evaluation. A document trail should allow each interpretation to be traced to original clauses, a conditional accounting memo, reporting assertions, downstream ICFR/CAM evidence and rival explanations. The qualitative goal is not to maximize an LLM's F1; it is to observe how professional reviewers negotiate category boundaries and what the system makes easier or more difficult to justify. A supplementary controlled experiment may compare A0 closed-book LLM, A1 hybrid RAG, A2 independent verifier, A3 specialist multi-agent and optional A4 graph retrieval after a protected human gold protocol is complete. In the current project all those arms are **NOT RUN**.

The proposed qualitative field extension would observe or interview accounting policy specialists, internal auditors, contract managers, IT risk personnel and audit engagement leaders in approximately three to five organizations, subject to access and ethics approval. The intent is process tracing rather than assuming interview counts guarantee theoretical saturation. Interviews and actual decision records are necessary to move from publicly observed regulatory exchanges to claims about how agentic systems redistribute professional judgment in practice.

## 6. Discussion: a potential contribution to AOS

The proposed construct of **evidentiary boundary work** is analytically distinguishable from classification accuracy. It describes five boundary transitions: *contractual to accounting* (promises acquire recognition categories); *accounting to controllable* (judgments acquire specified process evidence); *controllable to publicly reportable* (control and audit communication thresholds differ); *evidence to delegated decision* (retrieval output remains conditional until reviewed); and *financial assurance to organizational value* (risk predictions are not realized impacts). In each transition, evidence may be preserved, transformed, contested, missing or legitimately outside scope. The central theoretical question is what organizational practices determine whether this translation is accepted as auditable.

The distinctive extension to Kohler, Pochet and Le Manh (2021) is not another description of IFRS 15 ambiguity; it is the proposed observation of delegated computer-assisted boundary decisions that must be independently challenged. The extension to Power (1996) is not to claim that AI automatically improves auditability, but to investigate how it configures evidentiary objects and controls the conditions under which professional conclusions can be made. The extension to Baird and Maruping (2021) is a concrete setting in which delegation rights, sources of authority and refusal to infer may become observable in accounting work. These are **proposed theoretical contributions**, not established results from a three-document pilot.

AOS remains a plausible but demanding journal because it prioritizes substantive organizational theory over a software benchmark or a regulator-comment frequency table. An alternative outlet for a completed technical agent benchmark is *Journal of Information Systems* or *International Journal of Accounting Information Systems*. The contract-first paper currently under development in the researcher's AOS project already concerns documentary risk translation across CAM and ICFR; submitting two overlapping papers without clearly distinct datasets, constructs and contributions could result in duplicate-publication concerns. This version must therefore either be integrated as a defined agentic interpretive sub-study of the existing AOS manuscript or establish genuinely distinct field evidence, sampling and theoretical propositions before independent submission.

## 7. Limitations, ethics, rights and submission gates

The current pilot has three purposively selected SEC comment-response records and ten preliminary analytic units. It is not a sample of high-tech customer contracts. No original customer agreement has been authenticated against those records, no original auditor CAM or operating-control evidence has been joined, and no ICFR deficiency or monetary outcome has been verified. The three firms also differ in accounting basis; any statistical pooled inference from their disclosures would be inappropriate. The software screening tests use a three-document entirely synthetic fixture and are not evidence of contract interpretation accuracy. CUAD legal labels do not establish IFRS compliance, and a contract's appearance in CUAD does not demonstrate that its issuer recognizes revenue under IFRS.

Public filing does not eliminate the need to review permissions on third-party copies or confidential annexes. Contract content, identifiable personal data, client files, private Golden Q&A labels and unreleased code must not be uploaded to an open repository. This project's public replication package deliberately distributes software, original metadata, coding paraphrases and synthetic fixtures—not original text from the researcher's private Drive. Applicable IIA, IFRS and PCAOB standards must be checked for effective dates and jurisdiction; none confers authority for an LLM to issue an audit opinion.

Before a submission claim, freeze 50 independently verified real agreements; complete issuer-year/accounting framework checks; recheck relevant source spans; undertake independent coding and negative-case memos; link original revenue notes, Item 9A and CAM reports where available; observe actual organizational interpretation or agent use; and produce a transparent data/rights and reviewer-decision ledger. Retain failures and inaccessible cases to avoid selecting only convenient documentary traces. Results, conclusions and theoretical claims should then be rewritten using admitted evidence, rather than retrofitting the present conceptual paper with fabricated counts.

## 8. Conclusion

An agentic internal-audit system offers a way to trace high-tech revenue commitments across commercial, accounting and governance representations, but the quality of that trace depends on evidentiary boundaries as much as model capability. Three actual SEC comment-response documents make the difficulties of software obligation classification, temporal revenue recognition, standalone selling price and documentation governance visible, while revealing why a regulator's question does not constitute a control finding. The contract-first selection protocol and standards-aware candidate engine provide a reproducible starting point for examining 50 actual customer agreements when their source text and SEC identities have been obtained. At present, the paper supports a feasibility assessment and an explicit research agenda—not a validated 50-contract case study or causal argument about the benefits of agentic AI. The next empirical contribution must follow the verified contract and professional decision process before claims about CAM/ICFR integration or six-capital value can be sustained.
